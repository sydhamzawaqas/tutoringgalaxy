Phase 3 · Inspiration · 8 Oct 2026

Calm and academic, not AI-gradient.

A moodboard for the new tutoringgalaxy.com: 10 references from real sites and Dribbble, 4 colour palettes built from ColorHunt with WCAG-checked tokens, and 4 Google Fonts pairings. Read it alongside the current-state audit.

How this was made: dribbble.com, colorhunt.co and fonts.google.com were blocked from the research environment. Every inspiration URL below comes from web-search results, and the notes on them are based on search snippets, not on looking at the pages. Open each link before the design review. Palette hex codes come from a public GitHub list of ColorHunt palette codes. Font families and weights were checked against Google's own METADATA.pb files on GitHub.

Recommended palette

A. Scholar Navy & Old Gold. Ink #041C32 · brand navy #0F4C81 (primary) · old gold #ECB365 (highlight only) · paper #F7F4EC. Text on paper is 15.7:1 and the button label is 8.9:1.

Recommended type

Newsreader

Figtree for body text and interface

JetBrains Mono · x² + 5x + 6 = 0

Noto Nastaliq Urdu and Noto Naskh Arabic as fallbacks for Urdu and Arabic.

Design principles

  1. One primary action. "Book a free trial" is the only solid button on any screen. WhatsApp is the single secondary action, and nothing else competes with them.
  2. Calm academic, not AI-gradient. Use flat colour, paper backgrounds and navy ink. Gold covers less than 5% of a screen. No purple, no glass, no gradients.
  3. Real people over illustrations. Use real tutor photos, names and results. Where we don't have them yet, leave the space empty rather than invent them.
  4. Let type do the work. A serif for headlines, a sans for the interface, and mono with tabular figures for maths and prices.
  5. Few, strict rules. Two corner radii (6px for controls, 12px for cards), one spacing scale and a 7-section homepage.

Section 2

Ten inspirations

Seven live sites and three Dribbble shots. Each card lists what to borrow and what to avoid. The live sites were chosen for how they work (tutor cards, curriculum structure, parent reporting, AI practice), not just for how they look.

01Live site

MyTutor: city landing pages

www.mytutor.co.uk/united-kingdom/cardiff

UK GCSE/A Level tutoring for parents, which is the closest model to our business.

Borrow

  • Tutor cards that lead with price per hour, university and reviews, followed by a free 15-minute "meet the tutor" call. Our free trial maps onto this directly.
  • Short trust lines that make one specific claim each ("1 in 8 applicants make the cut", a Trustpilot score), not a grid of badges.
  • Real per-city pages for local SEO. They show we can do city pages without thousands of thin templates.

Avoid

  • Its claims vary between versions of its own pages (price floor, rating). We publish only numbers we can prove and keep them in one place.

02Live site

Varsity Tutors: subject tutor directory

www.varsitytutors.com/t/math

A mature 1-to-1 marketplace with dense, credential-led tutor profiles.

Borrow

  • Tutor profile cards that show degree, institution, years of experience and test scores. We can use O/A Level grades and university the same way.
  • Subject hubs (/t/math, /t/science) that drill down to topics. This is a good model for our Subjects pages.

Avoid

  • "Talk to our team for pricing" hides the price. We publish our PKR tiers openly.
  • Its 250+ subjects make the catalogue sprawl. We show our 21 subjects grouped by curriculum.

03Live site

Atom Learning: 11+ prep

www.atomlearning.com/11-plus-prep

Parent-first copy ("Take the stress out of the 11+") with progress reporting as the proof.

Borrow

  • A hero that names the parent's worry, then the outcome, in one line.
  • Progress reporting (score by subject and topic, comparison with peers) as the core product promise. This fits our parent progress report.
  • Scale proof ("1,000+ schools") sits next to an offer for families who need help paying, which builds goodwill.

Avoid

  • Its own claim that its papers are "the best match on the market" is unverifiable. We avoid superlatives.

Save My Exams: IGCSE hub

www.savemyexams.com/igcse/

The best information structure in the sector for exam board → subject → topic. These are the same curricula our families take.

Borrow

  • Navigation by level → subject → exam board (Cambridge, Edexcel), which mirrors how parents search ("CIE IGCSE Maths tutor").

Brilliant: maths

brilliant.org/math/

The benchmark for "learn by doing" interactive practice with a built-in AI tutor (Koji).

Borrow

  • The AI tutor guides step by step and doesn't reveal the answer. Use the same rule for our Gemini practice area.
  • Learning paths (Foundational → Advanced) are a clean model for our curriculum-aligned practice sets.

Khanmigo for parents

www.khanmigo.ai/parents

Shows how to sell an AI tutor to parents: safety, visibility and "it doesn't do the homework for them".

Borrow

  • Parents can see their child's AI chats and get alerts. This becomes a trust feature for our /app area.
  • Says "Socratic, not answer-giving" explicitly, which answers the cheating worry.
  • Interactive diagrams built on Gemini, the same model family we plan to use.

Avoid

  • US-only and age-gated. Do not copy its eligibility or pricing model.

07Live site

Synthesis Tutor

www.synthesis.com/tutor

Premium positioning for an AI tutor, with a strong origin story and a dedicated testimonials page.

Borrow

  • One clear product story (where the method came from and how it adapts) instead of a list of features.
  • Testimonials collected on one dedicated page, not scattered across the homepage.
  • Continuous micro-assessment explained in plain language for parents.

Avoid

  • Superlatives like "superhuman" and "99.99% of students" are unverifiable, which is exactly the trust risk flagged in our audit.
  • Confusing pricing with many tiers. Keep ours to three.

Section 3

Four palettes

Each palette is a real ColorHunt palette (4 colours), plus one or two added colours where it needed them. Each one is turned into semantic tokens for light and dark mode. Contrast is calculated with the WCAG 2.x formula: 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for interface elements. The previews below use each palette's own tokens.

Four font pairings

All are free Google Fonts and load from the Google Fonts CSS API. If the fonts don't load, the specimens fall back to system serif, sans and mono. For the production site, self-host the fonts with next/font and load only the weights we use.

Urdu fallback: Noto Nastaliq Urdu (wght 400–700). Arabic fallback: Noto Naskh Arabic (wght 400–700).

An editorial serif made for screens gives the "established school" feel. Figtree is a friendly geometric sans that is very legible at small mobile sizes and less generic than Inter or Poppins. The mono font gives clean maths and tabular prices.

Checked against Google Fonts METADATA.pb: Newsreader: variable, wght 200–800, opsz 6–72, roman and italic, Latin / Latin Ext / Vietnamese. Figtree: variable, wght 300–900, roman and italic, Latin / Latin Ext. JetBrains Mono: wght 100–800, Latin / Cyrillic / Greek.
